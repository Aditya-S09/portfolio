Selected case studies

Cross-border procurement platform

US importers source from India over spreadsheets, email and WhatsApp, with no audit trail and no view of the factory floor. A static purchase order never matches production, so one missing customs document can hold a whole shipment. I mapped the journey in five stages and found the white space nobody owns: Flexport runs logistics, Alibaba runs discovery, the big ERPs run finance, but the middle from PO to shipment is empty. I scoped an MVP for that middle: shared PO collaboration, milestone updates with photo proof, an auditable change history and a customs-doc checklist that hands straight to the freight forwarder.

A competitive teardown of Flexport, Alibaba and Coupa, a five-stage journey map and a prioritised MVP.Read the full case study

Unnati, savings for rural women

Rural women are India's largest untapped savings market, but literacy, trust and access keep them out, and their income is irregular. A screen-first app would not land, because trust in a village is carried by people and not interfaces. So I designed Unnati as phygital: digital rails with a human front door. A voice-first app in the local language, goal-based savings jars, ₹20-a-day UPI AutoPay and digital gold, onboarded by local Sakhi agents, with kirana stores as the cash-in and cash-out points.

A full PRD with a go-to-market built on Self-Help-Group networks and a phased roadmap to micro-credit.Read the full strategy

Daily risk from monthly marks

Illiquid portfolios are marked once a month, which hides how violently they move day to day and understates the tail risk that regulators and investors watch. That daily structure turns out to be reconstructable, because it leaves a signature in the monthly marks once you model the hierarchy and the correlations together. I built a three-stage, hierarchy-constrained diffusion model with cross-asset attention on NIFTY50 equities, benchmarked against Denton, Brownian-Bridge and GARCH(1,1).

Monthly marks understate tail risk by 33%. The model cut drawdown error 69% and correlation error 56%.Read on SSRN

Quick commerce's quality crisis

As ten-minute delivery scaled, quality complaints surged, wrong items, damaged goods and expired stock, putting a fast-growing market's trust and roughly ₹5,000 Cr of revenue at risk. It reads like a warehouse problem but it is really a workflow one: returns are a dead end instead of a signal, and the app never asks the right question at the right moment. I proposed three fixes the support, QC and app teams could ship: refund triage that routes by failure type, a dark-store QC checkpoint before dispatch and app-side design that catches defects at the door rather than the doorstep.

A root-cause breakdown and three workflow redesigns three teams could ship.Read the article
